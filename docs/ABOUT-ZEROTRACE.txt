================================================================================
APEX ZEROTRACE
An encrypted vault that can destroy its own key
Version 0.18.6
================================================================================

WHAT IT IS

ZeroTrace stores your files inside a single encrypted container. Nobody can
read what is inside without your password, and the container does not reveal
what sort of thing it holds: names, sizes and dates are encrypted alongside the
contents.

Two things make it unusual.

It can be told to destroy itself. Set a deadline, and if you stop checking in
before it passes, the key is overwritten and everything inside becomes
permanently unreadable.

And its key can be split. Instead of one password standing between an attacker
and your files, the key is divided into three pieces of which any two are
needed. Somebody who steals your drive and guesses your password has one piece.

It is written in Rust, runs on Windows, macOS and Linux, and is free software
under the Apache 2.0 license.


================================================================================
FEATURES
================================================================================

--- ENCRYPTION AND STORAGE -----------------------------------------------------

Authenticated encryption
    XChaCha20-Poly1305 by default; AES-256-GCM where a compliance rule requires
    it. Every piece of stored data carries a tag proving it has not been
    altered, so a modified file is refused rather than quietly decrypted into
    nonsense.

Encrypted metadata
    Filenames, folder paths, sizes and timestamps are encrypted with the
    contents. The container shows its total size and nothing else about what is
    in it.

Slow key derivation
    Argon2id turns your password into a key, deliberately using time and memory
    so that testing millions of guesses is expensive rather than instant. The
    settings are stored inside the authenticated part of the container, so an
    attacker cannot hand you back a weakened copy.

Chunked storage with integrity checking
    Large files are stored in one-megabyte pieces, each encrypted separately,
    with a fingerprint over all of them. Damage is localised and detectable
    rather than silently corrupting.

Compression
    Files are compressed before encryption where that helps, and stored as-is
    where it does not. Already-compressed formats are recognized and skipped.

Constant memory use
    A five gigabyte file is read, encrypted and written a piece at a time.
    Memory use does not grow with file size, so a file larger than your RAM is
    not a problem.

Progress reporting
    Adding and extracting large files show a progress bar and keep the window
    responsive.


--- SPLIT-KEY PROTECTION -------------------------------------------------------

Two of three components
    The key that opens a vault is divided into three pieces: your password, and
    two small token files you keep elsewhere. Any two open the vault.

    Two of three rather than three of three, deliberately. Requiring all three
    would mean any single loss is permanent: a dead computer, a lost USB stick,
    a forgotten password. Two of three still defeats a stolen drive and
    survives losing any one piece.

Protection against a stolen drive
    An attacker who removes your drive, copies the vault, and correctly guesses
    your password still holds only one piece and needs two. Guessing for a
    thousand years does not change that arithmetic.

Recovery without the password
    Lost your password? The two tokens open the vault between them.

Instant to set up
    Enrolling rewrites 48 bytes. A vault of any size converts in the time it
    takes to derive one key; nothing stored is re-encrypted.


--- THE DEADMAN SWITCH ---------------------------------------------------------

Cryptographic erasure
    When the deadline passes, the key is overwritten and confirmed gone by
    reading the bytes back. This is more reliable than deleting or overwriting
    the files themselves, because modern storage relocates data invisibly and
    may keep copies you cannot address. A key that no longer exists cannot be
    recovered from any of them.

Presence scoring, not a simple timer
    Evidence that you are around is weighted. Entering your password scores
    full marks. Signs that the computer is merely switched on, connected, or
    being typed at are capped well below the threshold, because a burglar at
    your desk produces all of them.

Deadlines that resist manipulation
    Time asleep still counts, so closing a laptop does not postpone anything.
    Winding the clock back does not either: elapsed time is taken as the larger
    of two independent measures.

Warning states
    A vault moves through NORMAL, WARNING, CRITICAL and ARMED rather than
    jumping from fine to gone, and checking in at any point before destruction
    is authorized returns it to normal.

Two-phase authorization
    Reaching the deadline commits a decision; destruction then proceeds as a
    separate step. An interrupted destruction resumes rather than reverting.

Mandatory dry run
    Arming is locked until you have seen a dry run listing exactly what would
    happen. It changes nothing.

Honest destruction reports
    Afterwards you are told what was verified, what was best effort, and what
    was not attempted. Nothing is claimed that was not observed.


--- REMOTE CUSTODY -------------------------------------------------------------

A component held off the machine
    A custodian, kept on another computer or a network share, holds one of the
    three key pieces. It returns that piece only while you keep checking in,
    and destroys it when you stop.

Survives losing control of the computer
    This is the only part of ZeroTrace that somebody sitting at your machine
    cannot switch off. Everything else runs there, and whoever controls the
    machine can close it. The custodian does not.

Check-ins that an attacker cannot forge
    Requests are signed with a key derived from your password, existing only
    long enough to sign one. Somebody holding your computer but not your
    password cannot keep the deadline open. Somebody who cracks the password
    after the deadline finds the piece already destroyed.

Graceful when unreachable
    A custodian that is not mounted today does not stop you opening a vault
    whose remaining pieces still add up.


--- TAMPER EVIDENCE ------------------------------------------------------------

Hash-chained audit log
    Every event is sealed to the one before it. Editing, deleting or reordering
    any entry breaks every entry after it.

Detection of removed entries
    A chain cannot notice records removed from its own end, because a shortened
    chain is still valid. A separate state journal records how long the log
    should be, which catches exactly that.

Recorded interruptions
    A watcher that was killed, crashed, or had its window closed is recorded on
    the next start, along with how long the vault went unwatched. This cannot
    be prevented on a machine somebody else controls, but it cannot be done
    quietly either.

Integrity verification
    One command decrypts every stored piece and checks it against its
    fingerprint, finding disk damage as well as deliberate tampering.


--- OPERATION ------------------------------------------------------------------

Three programs
    A desktop window, a command-line tool, and a background service that
    watches deadlines. All three work on the same vaults.

Background service
    Runs independently of the window. Start it, arm it, and stop it from the
    desktop, or install it to restart when you log in without needing
    administrator rights.

Multiple vaults
    Each vault is tracked separately, with its own policy, deadline and
    watcher. The window lists your other vaults and flags any that are not
    being watched or whose watcher stopped unexpectedly.

Password strength enforcement
    New passwords must be at least fifteen characters. A phrase of unrelated
    words joined by dashes is encouraged rather than symbols and digits
    demanded, because complexity rules produce passwords that are worse and
    harder to remember.

Failed attempt handling
    Repeated wrong passwords are met with escalating delays. Optional
    destruction after a set number of failures exists but is off by default,
    and refuses to be set below three.

Panic controls
    Panic lock clears anything held in memory and leaves your data untouched.
    Panic destroy is permanent, and requires the same key pieces as opening the
    vault, so somebody who cannot read it cannot destroy it either.

Threshold recovery for organizations
    A master key can be split among several custodians so that a quorum can
    recover it. It deliberately does not survive a committed destruction.

Signed remote commands
    An organization can lock, require a check-in, shorten a deadline or destroy
    a vault, using signed and replay-resistant commands. No command can weaken
    a vault, and none can read its contents.

Documentation
    Full manuals for both the window and the command line, written for somebody
    with no background in encryption, each with a glossary.


================================================================================
HOW IT COMPARES
================================================================================

ZeroTrace does not use better cryptography than the established tools. It uses
the same well-reviewed building blocks. What differs is what sits on top.

VeraCrypt
    Mature, widely used, independently audited, and offers hidden volumes for
    plausible deniability, which ZeroTrace does not. It is a container you
    mount as a drive. It has no deadline, no split key and no self-destruction.
    If your requirement is a strong encrypted volume from a proven tool,
    VeraCrypt is the more conservative choice.

BitLocker and FileVault
    Full-disk encryption built into the operating system, transparent in daily
    use, and protecting a powered-off machine well. They protect the disk, not
    individual vaults, and offer no deadline, no split key and no destruction.
    They also depend on the vendor's key escrow arrangements.

Cryptomator
    File-level encryption aimed at cloud storage, encrypting filenames as
    ZeroTrace does, open source and audited. It is designed for syncing rather
    than for deadlines or key splitting.

7-Zip and similar archivers
    Strong AES encryption and good compression. Filenames are only hidden if
    you remember a specific option. No key management, no deadline, no
    integrity model beyond the archive itself.

Shamir tools such as ssss
    Split a secret among several holders, which ZeroTrace also does. They are
    building blocks rather than vaults: no storage, no deadline, no interface.

Dead man's switch services
    Web services that notify contacts or release messages if you stop
    responding. They deliver information; they do not destroy data, and they
    require trusting a third party with whatever they hold.

Secure deletion tools
    Overwrite files in place. On modern storage this is unreliable, because
    filesystems and drives relocate data invisibly. ZeroTrace destroys the key
    instead, which is why it can make a claim about copies it cannot reach.

WHERE ZEROTRACE IS DIFFERENT

The combination, not any single part. A vault whose deadline destroys the key
rather than the file; whose key is split so that a stolen drive plus a guessed
password is not enough; and where one piece can live somewhere the attacker
does not control.

WHERE IT IS BEHIND

It has not been independently audited. VeraCrypt and Cryptomator have. For a
security tool this is the single most important difference, and it counts
against ZeroTrace.

It is pre-1.0 and written by one author. Forensic testing has been started, not
finished: destruction has been checked once on a USB stick, where the key could
not be recovered from the raw disk afterwards. That is one run on one kind of
storage, and no attempt has been made at physical media recovery.

Hardware key binding, using a TPM or a FIDO2 key, is designed but not built.
Without it, nothing binds a vault to one physical computer, and an attacker who
holds your drive can restore an older copy of its files without that being
detected.


================================================================================
WHAT IT CANNOT DO
================================================================================

Recover a lost password
    If a vault is not split-key protected and the password is gone, it is
    unreadable for ever. Nobody can help.

Protect copies made earlier
    The key lives inside the container, so a backup taken before destruction
    carries its own key and still opens. Destroying a vault protects that file,
    not copies of it.

Defend a computer that is already compromised while a vault is open
    At that moment the files are readable by you, and therefore by anything
    running as you.

Stop somebody closing the watcher
    Nothing can, on a machine they control. What defends a vault against that
    person is key protection, not the deadline: stopping the watcher gives them
    unlimited time, and against a split key unlimited time is worth nothing.

Detect a rolled-back drive
    Somebody holding your disk can restore an older copy of any file on it.
    Detecting that needs hardware support this version does not have.

Hide how compressible your files are
    Compression happens before encryption, so the container's size reveals
    something about the nature of its contents.


================================================================================
STATUS
================================================================================

Version 0.18.6. Free software under the Apache 2.0 license.

251 automated tests. Every capability is reported by the program itself as
verified, best effort, not supported, or not implemented, and that report is
generated from the code so it cannot drift from what the software actually
does. Run `zt security audit` to see it.

Three things stand between this and a 1.0 security claim: hardware key binding,
forensic validation that a destroyed vault genuinely cannot be recovered, and
an independent cryptographic review. None has been done.

Treat it accordingly: promising, honest about its limits, and not yet something
to stake irreplaceable data on without keeping a backup you control.
